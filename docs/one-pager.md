# What's Your Take?

*A pop-up civic guestbook from We (ARE) the People*

Under a shaded canopy, a table asks one question. This season's: **"What would
you remind an American in 2075?"** There is nothing to sign up for, nothing to
buy, and no side to join. You can write your answer on a card and decorate it
with markers and stamps, type it at the table, or submit it from your phone.
You can also just watch. Every way of participating — including not
participating — is welcome.

Responses are anonymous by design — and shared publicly, and we say so right
on the card. No name, email, or phone number is ever collected; there is
nowhere to put one. Cards drop into a bucket unread; digital responses can
only be submitted by people physically at the table, so the day's collection
belongs to the place it came from. A small lit spot on the table lets anyone
photograph their own card, framed by the prompt, if they choose to share it
themselves. That choice is theirs alone.

Beside the guestbook sits a second, simpler invitation: three obviously-fake
dollar bills and one question — *where do you want these to go?* Write it,
staple it to the board, and watch an unpolled, uncoached picture of a
community's priorities accumulate over the day. Participants leave with a
sticker and a pointer to It's Our Money, a companion project on where the
real dollars actually go.

**What it produces.** When the table closes, every response — card and screen —
is gathered and distilled into the day's common themes, alongside a few
anonymous quotes in participants' own words: a portrait of what one place, on
one day, wanted remembered. And because a summary you can't check is just a
claim, the full anonymous collection is published with it — browsable and
downloadable, so anyone can read the sources or draw their own portrait. The
physical cards are preserved toward a traveling exhibit and, eventually, a
book. Down the road, we want to project
these reflections onto buildings at night — the city reading its own
handwriting.

**Why.** Most public invitations to "engage" arrive pre-sorted: pick a side,
prove your identity, brace for a fight. This table tests a different premise —
that given two minutes, a marker, and no audience, people say things to one
another across every line we're told divides us. The point isn't the answer.
It's the experience of being asked, and the quiet discovery of how much
company you have.

**Footprint.** One 10×10 canopy, two tables, art supplies, a small crew of
volunteers. Self-contained; no venue power or internet required for the core
experience. We come as a community activation, not a vendor.
