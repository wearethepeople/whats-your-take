# What's Your Take? — The Annual Ritual

*Concept doc. Companion to `spec.md` (the how) and `one-pager.md` (the pitch).
This is the shape of the thing over a year: one question, a season of quiet
gathering, and a dated reveal that becomes a recurring civic practice.*

Status: concept, 2026-07-24. Ritual mechanics settled in conversation; the
inner workings of the prompt-raise are designed in separate material (see
Open Questions).

---

## The Core Invitation

Once a season, one open question. You stand at the table for two minutes and
answer it honestly — not for an audience, not against anyone, just your own
true take. Then you wait, with everyone else, to find out what we all said.

## The Experience

A person encounters the table. There's a single prompt — no menu, no sorting,
no politics to perform. They answer, digitally or on a card. A short-lived
claim code proves only that they were here, today; the host promotes their
draft into the corpus by that code, and nothing links the code to the
response beyond the event and a coarse hour. The response is theirs, given
away — anonymous, headed for a shared portrait they can't yet see.

What they *don't* get is everyone else's answers. That withholding is the
point, not a limitation: seeing the responses before you give yours corrupts
the take, the same way seeing a survey's results before you answer would.
The only thing that moves in public during the season is a count — *N people
have taken their turn* — the single honest live mirror.

Co-located but wholly separate, a secondary experience (*It's Our Money*)
offers a different jolt: allocate a small, make-believe budget, feel what
agency feels like from the inside rather than from a candidate's stage. It
shares the table's physical space and none of its machinery — no season, no
corpus, no reveal. It's named here only so the two aren't confused; it has
its own design and its own rules.

## The Civic Mirror

After the season closes and the host reviews, the responses become a
**synthesis** — a portrait of what a community wanted to say — and a
**browsable, exportable corpus**. Public means public: anything readable is
copyable, and the point-of-participation grant said so plainly.

This is not published quietly on a Tuesday. It **premieres** on a fixed,
announced date — for the sake of argument, Independence Day — as a civic
event: *on this day we'll learn what people want to remind our future
country.* The withholding all season was in service of this one shared moment
of finding out together. Where feasible, the premiere is projected in more
than one city at once; but it is designed to be complete in a single room, so
that rain, a dead livestream, or three cities instead of twenty never turns
the reveal into a failure. The spectacle is upside. The meaning survives one
projector.

## The Argument

Every part of this models a claim about civic life:

- **The single honest question** models that we can be asked what we think
  without being sorted first.
- **The withholding** models patience and non-contamination — that a
  collective picture is worth more when no one's answer was bent by seeing
  the others.
- **The dated reveal** models that a community can look at itself all at once,
  in public, and find something other than a fight.
- **The recurrence** is the real argument. This is not an event; it's a
  **practice** — an annual reckoning a community comes to expect. The first
  premiere is chapter one of an institution, not a one-time rocket.

## Choosing the Next Question

The ritual renews itself in public. As a season nears its end — announced off
the table, through signage-driven newsletter and social follows, never by
capturing anyone at the moment they participate — people propose prompts and,
in a short window, **raise** the ones they want most. The raise counts stay
**sealed** until the next prompt is revealed.

Sequencing:

1. Season closes.
2. **Premiere night** carries *only* the synthesis and the opened corpus.
   Nothing competes with it.
3. About a week later — after the reveal has had its own moment, and any
   coverage its own news cycle — the **next season's prompt is revealed**,
   with the sealed raise resolved. A second quiet beat: here's what we said,
   and here's what we'll ask next.

Letting people raise prompts without capturing any PII is a genuine identity
problem — how do you know a raise came from a distinct real person without
asking who they are? — and it is designed in separate material. At this
altitude what matters is that the raise is low-stakes by design, so its first
real-world test is forgiving: the only thing at stake is which question gets
asked next.

## Production Considerations

- **One operator.** The premiere must carry only one thing (the reveal), or
  none of it lands. Everything else lives on its own clock: the season, the
  raise, the secondary experience.
- **The pull-only bridge.** No PII is captured at the table, ever — so there
  is no path to retarget a participant. Every downstream connection
  (newsletter, social, the raise) is a *pull*: the person opts in on their
  own, from signage, outside the act of submitting. This protects trust and
  bounds reach; the design job is making that voluntary bridge inviting.
- **Design to degrade gracefully.** Multi-city projection and press coverage
  are upside, never load-bearing. You own the next-prompt reveal regardless,
  so the second beat exists whether or not the articles show up.
- **Never a funnel.** What's Your Take is complete in itself — worth doing on
  its own terms. It is never instrumentalized as acquisition or a growth
  channel for anything downstream. Responses never leave the corpus for
  another system; participation is never a conversion event.
- **The date is a promise.** A public premiere date becomes part of the civic
  compact. Build slack, or announce a window rather than a hard pin, until
  you're certain you can hold it.

## Open Questions

- **The raise mechanic's guts.** Letting people raise prompts without
  capturing PII is a hard identity question, designed in separate material —
  named here, opened there.
- **Prompt curation.** Open submission invites junk and partisan bait — the
  one contaminant engineered out of the table. What curation gate keeps the
  pool open-emotional and non-partisan before anything reaches a raise, and
  who holds that pen?
- **Premiere production.** What's the smallest complete version (one room),
  and what's the graceful path up to many cities?
- **End of chapter, or first of a rite.** Confirmed direction: recurring
  annual ritual. The first premiere should be framed as the first instance of
  a practice, not a finale.
